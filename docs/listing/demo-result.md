## Summary Judgment

The key question: **Will the substantial order backlog translate into earnings and cash flow?**

Hanwha Engine's backlog and first-quarter profitability are attractive. For a new investment over 12–24 months, however, the appropriate stance is **a staged approach conditional on earnings confirmation**. Q1 2026 backlog of KRW 5.24 trillion and an operating margin of 14.9% provide strong evidence. At the reference price, the stock trades at approximately 22.4 times FY2025 earnings and 7.0 times book value. Product-level margins, aftermarket revenue recurrence, and annual backlog conversion have not been verified through disclosures.

As of July 10, the share price was below its 5-, 20-, 60-, 120-, and 200-day moving averages and failed the Minervini rules. Recognize the favorable business cycle while checking profitability, utilization, capital expenditure, and Hanwha Ocean concentration from Q2 onward.

## Business and Thesis

Hanwha Engine is a Hanwha Group operating company producing large low-speed marine engines, engine parts and aftermarket services, selective catalytic reduction systems (SCR), and diesel power-generation equipment. The thesis centers on higher-priced and environmentally compliant vessel orders converting into engine deliveries and profits, and capacity expansion relieving production constraints.

Official Q1 2026 backlog was KRW 5.243T, or 3.02 times FY2025 consolidated revenue. This is a simple backlog-to-revenue calculation. It does not establish profit visibility after delivery timing, costs, exchange rates, and cancellations. The backlog should not automatically be treated as three years of revenue or high-margin business.

## What the Latest Results Say

- **FY2025:** Consolidated revenue of KRW 1,371.1B, operating profit of KRW 130.1B, a 9.5% operating margin, and net income of KRW 173.8B. The comparative FY2024 revenue disclosure is on a standalone basis, so a mechanical consolidated year-over-year growth calculation is inappropriate.
- **Q1 2026:** Consolidated revenue of KRW 345.2B, operating profit of KRW 51.4B, a 14.9% operating margin, and net income of KRW 52.9B. The margin improvement is positive, but undisclosed product and contract profitability prevents treating one quarter as proof of a structural margin change.
- **Cash flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was KRW +330.3B, investing cash flow KRW −161.1B, and year-end cash KRW 276.7B. Q1 contract liabilities of KRW 665.8B may indicate future revenue, but contract-specific timing is unavailable.
- **Capacity:** FY2025 utilization of 100.7%, investment in progress of KRW 41.5B, and planned investment of KRW 350.4B across reporting periods 28–30 suggest potential bottleneck relief. Improved ROIC requires confirmation of actual spending, utilization, and cash conversion.

## Governance and Structure

- Hanwha Impact, the largest shareholder, held 32.77% as of December 31, 2025. Hanwha Ocean offers group synergies, while pricing, volumes, and receivable terms can depend heavily on a small number of affiliated companies.
- The annual report identifies seven directors: three executive, one other non-executive, and three outside directors. CEO/chair separation and specific capital-allocation policies were not independently confirmed in this dataset and require rechecking.
- Market data shows 21,607 treasury shares, or 0.03%, which is not a major dilution or cancellation variable. The available evidence is insufficient to assign a shareholder-return premium to dividend or buyback policies.

## Catalysts

1. Operating margins from Q2 onward remain close to Q1's 14.9%, with evidence of profitable backlog conversion.
2. Expansion improves deliveries, output, and cash conversion despite utilization exceeding 100%.
3. Orders and revenue diversify beyond Hanwha Ocean, or affiliate transaction terms become more transparent.
4. Quantitative disclosure of SEAM purchase-price allocation, net debt, service network, and aftermarket synergies.
5. Dual-fuel, methanol-engine, and SCR mix translates into disclosed product or contract-group earnings.

## Risks and Disconfirming Evidence

1. Lower shipbuilding orders or newbuild prices, and expanding Chinese capacity, could pressure new-order pricing and margins.
2. Concentration in Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy Industries' Geoje yard transmits changes in customer production and orders.
3. Currency exposure is managed through natural hedges, forwards, and swaps. Q1 dollar forward-sale notional of USD 1.764B and aggregate derivative valuations nevertheless warrant monitoring during sharp currency moves.
4. Planned investment of KRW 350.4B could burden cash flow and ROIC if executed after peak demand.
5. Warranty provisions of KRW 11.66B, four defendant lawsuits totaling KRW 17.1B, and advance-payment-refund and performance guarantees require continued monitoring.

## Uncomfortable Questions

1. How much of the KRW 5.24T backlog converts to revenue in each year, and are costs, exchange rates, and royalties already locked in?
2. Do dual-fuel, methanol, and SCR price premiums become contribution profit, or are they absorbed by licensing, materials, and expansion costs?
3. Is aftermarket genuinely recurring revenue that cushions the cycle, or volatile parts revenue following engine deliveries?
4. Does rising Hanwha Ocean exposure reflect group synergy or weakening external-customer diversification?
5. Are the KRW 350.4B investment plan and SEAM acquisition high-ROIC solutions to supply constraints, or capital-intensive expansion late in the cycle?

## Structured Stance

**Current stance: Monitor first; conditional staged buying.**

Backlog, Q1 profitability, environmentally compliant engines, and service optionality can support a 12–24-month upside thesis. Current multiples already embed substantial expectations, while the key questions about margins, aftermarket recurrence, and backlog quality remain unresolved in disclosures.

A new investment requires confirmation that high margins recur over the next two quarters, capex and SEAM do not impair cash flow, and Hanwha Ocean concentration does not intensify. Rapid normalization of margins below FY2025 levels because of weaker order pricing or demand—or a break below KRW 41,500 together with earnings downgrades—would invalidate the buying thesis.
